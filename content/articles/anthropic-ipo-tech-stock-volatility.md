# Anthropic IPO: Tech Stocks Slide Before the $1.4 Trillion Debut

The AI trade entered the week on the back foot. Nasdaq-100 futures fell 1.5% on Monday, S&P 500 futures slipped 0.7%, and Dow futures gave up 55 points as traders de-risked ahead of two events that will set the price of artificial intelligence for the next quarter: Nvidia's third-quarter results on Tuesday and Anthropic's initial public offering on Thursday.

Of the two, the IPO is the more consequential. Nvidia is a known quantity with a broken boardroom. Anthropic is the first frontier AI lab to ask public shareholders to underwrite the cost of building frontier models — and it is doing so at a valuation of up to $1.405 trillion, roughly a third higher than the $965 billion it agreed to in its May funding round.

According to an amended S-1 filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission and reporting by CNBC, the Claude maker will offer 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each. It plans to price on 18 November and begin trading on the Nasdaq on 19 November under the ticker **ANT**.

The combined $90.4 billion would edge past the $85.7 billion raised by SpaceX in its June listing, the current record. If the underwriters exercise the over-allotment option in full, the total crosses $100 billion, according to Moneycontrol. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the book, with 20 other banks in the syndicate.

## Why tech stocks fell before the IPO

Monday's weakness was concentrated in the companies most exposed to the AI capex cycle, not in anything Anthropic said. Nvidia fell about 4% in premarket trading, extending a 13% slide last week after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive amid a personal scandal and the board named chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim CEO. The stock is now roughly 32% below its October peak. Micron, Intel and AMD each dropped more than 5%, Dell lost 5% and Oracle fell nearly 4%.

Two more analysts cut Nvidia price targets on Monday morning. Mizuho moved to $220 from $250 and UBS to $240 from $300, both citing the loss of what one called the "Jensen/founder premium" and the management overhang created by a government investigation.

The damage was global. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.42% after third-quarter GDP unexpectedly shrank, South Korea's Kospi dropped 3.59% and China's CSI 300 lost 0.91%, while Europe held roughly flat. Last week, by contrast, the Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq each gained more than 2% — their best week since August. Monday looks less like a verdict on AI and more like a market taking risk off the table before it has to price two binary events.

That distinction matters for anyone trying to read the IPO itself. A weak tape is a headwind for new issues, but an oversubscribed book is what actually sets the clearing price.

## Demand: seven times covered, a fifth reserved for retail

Reports put investor demand at close to seven times the shares on offer. People familiar with the process told CNBC that the book is already covered at the $70 to $78 range on an allocable basis and that institutional demand exceeds what SpaceX attracted at the equivalent stage.

Anthropic is also targeting a retail allocation of about 20%, far above the 5% to 10% that is typical for a US listing of this size. That is partly a governance choice and partly a signal about the buyer base the company wants: individual holders are less likely than institutions to sell into the first drawdown.

The practical consequence is that most people who try to buy at the IPO price will not get shares. Allocation will be thin, and the size of the first-day pop will be decided by the free float rather than by the depth of the order book.

## The financials under a trillion-dollar price tag

The revenue line is real, and it is the strongest argument the bull case has.

Anthropic reported $33.24 billion of revenue for the nine months to 30 September, up roughly 1,200% from $2.55 billion a year earlier. Third-quarter revenue was about $17 billion, up 1,127% year over year. Growth is decelerating on a quarterly basis, however: revenue rose 47% sequentially in Q3, down from 144% in Q2 and 132% in Q1. The company also reported a $23.29 billion net loss for the quarter, and a $104 billion net loss for the nine-month period, most of which came from revaluing convertible notes and derivative liabilities rather than from operations.

The number that deserves the most attention sits further down the filing. Anthropic has signed purchase commitments and lease agreements with an aggregate committed spend of about $895.4 billion, "substantially all of which is related to compute and infrastructure." Compute and infrastructure expenses rose by $14.4 billion in the nine months, up 329% from a year earlier.

That is the leverage in the story. A company earning roughly $17 billion a quarter has committed something on the order of three years of current revenue to future compute — capacity it needs in order to serve demand that may or may not arrive on schedule. The bull case is that enterprise adoption compounds fast enough to absorb it. The bear case is that the commitments are fixed and the demand is not. Anthropic's enterprise base is growing quickly enough to keep the argument alive: customers spending more than $100,000 a year reached about 11,900, up from roughly 950.

## The customers are also the buyers

The $26 billion private placement is the part of this deal that will get the most scrutiny after the print. Alphabet, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC have all agreed to buy Class N common stock at the IPO price — roughly $10 billion each from Alphabet and Nvidia, about $2.5 billion from AMD, and $1 billion each from Microsoft, Micron and Broadcom.

On one reading, this is the strongest possible endorsement: the companies that supply Anthropic's chips, its cloud capacity and its distribution are willing to take equity at the same price as public investors. On another, it is circular. The firms selling Anthropic compute are financing the buyer of that compute, at a valuation they help set, in a transaction that does not carry a lock-up.

That last detail is the one to watch. Because the private placement shares are unregistered and not subject to underwriter lock-ups, they represent a supply overhang that the standard 180-day calendar does not capture. It will not matter on the first day. It will matter on the first drawdown.

## The risks Anthropic admits

The S-1 runs to more than 90 pages of risk factors, and the company is unusually direct about the ones it cannot quantify. Political headwinds are the most immediate: the midterm elections delivered a Democratic sweep in Congress, and several states passed ballot measures that could slow data-centre construction. Rising interest rates are making debt more expensive for the developers and neoclouds Anthropic depends on for capacity.

MarketWatch framed the problem as "uninsurable risks" — a useful phrase. There is no actuarial history for model-driven systemic events, no established liability regime for autonomous systems, and no clear way to price national-security exposure created by the models themselves. Investors are being asked to underwrite those gaps with a valuation multiple.

There is also the oldest tension in the company's story. Anthropic has positioned itself as the safety-conscious lab, and its founders wrote in the filing that they consider it their responsibility to help the world understand the risks their models create. A public company answers to a shareholder base that wants growth. The two duties will not always point in the same direction, and the filing gives management room to choose.

## What to watch this week

- **Tuesday, after the close:** Nvidia's Q3 results and the conference call. Analysts expect close to 100% year-over-year earnings and revenue growth; the questions will be about guidance, customer concentration and who runs the company next. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has already called for the board to look outside Nvidia for a chief executive.
- **Wednesday:** the pricing. Watch where Anthropic lands in the $70 to $78 band, whether the deal is upsized, and how much of the greenshoe is exercised. A print at the top with a full shoe is a stronger signal than the first-day pop.
- **Thursday:** the debut. The number that matters is not the opening trade but the close relative to the offer price, and whether volume holds into the afternoon.
- **The following weeks:** OpenAI's plans. It filed confidentially in June and has signalled a 2027 debut. A strong Anthropic print pulls that timetable forward; a weak one gives OpenAI a reason to wait.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the Anthropic IPO?

Anthropic is expected to price its offering on 18 November 2026 and begin trading on the Nasdaq on 19 November 2026.

### What is Anthropic's ticker symbol?

ANT, on the Nasdaq Global Select Market.

### What valuation is Anthropic targeting?

Between $1.3 trillion and $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis. At the top of the $70 to $78 range the company would be worth up to $1.405 trillion, up from the $965 billion valuation of its May funding round.

### Is this the biggest IPO ever?

The offering alone raises up to $64.4 billion, below SpaceX's $85.7 billion in June. Including the $26 billion private placement, total fundraising reaches about $90.4 billion — a record — and could exceed $100 billion with the over-allotment option.

### Can retail investors buy Anthropic at the IPO price?

Only through an allocation from a participating broker. Anthropic is targeting about 20% of the deal for retail, several times the usual share, but demand is running at roughly seven times the available stock. Everyone else can buy in the secondary market from 19 November.

### Why are tech stocks falling ahead of the IPO?

Three things at once: investors reducing exposure before two binary events, a governance shock at Nvidia that has removed roughly a third of its market value since October, and rising rates that raise the cost of the debt financing the AI build-out depends on. None of them is a direct verdict on Anthropic's business.

## The bottom line

Anthropic does not need an AI boom to complete this IPO. It needs a bid, and it has one. What it cannot control is the price the market puts on AI risk after Thursday, when the largest generation of AI capital spending in history has a public share price attached to it for the first time.

Two numbers get set this week: the price Anthropic prints, and the price investors are willing to pay for everything that looks like it.
